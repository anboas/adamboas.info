Sabre Systems growth strategy

Where to play, who matters, and what to sell first

The growth strategy is to turn Sabre's mission-systems credibility into a repeatable modernization operating model: identify funded friction, shape a practical technical offer, bring the right partner, and convert early proof into durable pipeline.

DAF

Primary wedge through EWAAC, Eglin, weapons, test, software, and data modernization.

4E

Enterprise scale through DISA, DLA, DHA, CDAO, MDA, cyber, data, and workflow pain.

NAVAIR

Proof base to translate into adjacent Navy, Marine Corps, Army, and joint modernization plays.

Strategy Market Customers Sabre Fit Offers First 30 Days Evidence Sources

Operating thesis

Sabre should become the integrator that converts mission friction into funded modernization.

Adam's role should not be "AI guy" or generic business development. It should be growth architecture: translating customer pain into a technical, contractual, and partner-backed path that Sabre can win and deliver.

Strategy in one sentence: use NAVAIR and APG as proof, Air Force as the near-term wedge, Fourth Estate as the scale field, and Cognition/Devin as the first partner-led modernization offer.
Strategy

Build a growth architecture function

Operate between account insight, technical solutioning, partner leverage, contract path, and delivery feedback. Do not become a deck factory.
Market

Prioritize buyers with pain, money, and channel

The best targets have a named mission friction, budget owner, buying path, and a reason Sabre can credibly move faster than incumbents.
Offer

Productize the Sabre + partner wedge

Turn broad capability into named modernization plays: agentic software, digital engineering, cATO evidence, test acceleration, and workflow modernization.
Proof

Use delivery evidence to create the next pursuit

Every pilot should produce metrics, artifacts, CPARS-quality proof, a follow-on path, and reusable language for the next customer.

Industry and market

The market is not short on priorities. It is short on conversion paths.

The defense market is saturated with modernization language. The edge is finding where customer urgency, budget authority, acquisition channel, and Sabre delivery credibility overlap.

Demand is broad. Buyer-ready demand is narrow.

Every service says AI, software, data, cyber, autonomy, and digital engineering matter. Strategy means finding the few places where pain, authority, and funding align.

Score signals by mission pain, money path, vehicle, Sabre right-to-win, partner leverage, and follow-on scale.

The money is split across experiments, operations, and programs.

RDT&E starts experiments, O&M scales enterprise services and sustainment, and procurement turns modernization into durable platform spend.

Match each offer to the color of money before shaping the capture story.

AI only matters when attached to a hard workflow.

Customers will buy outcomes: backlog burn-down, test evidence, ATO speed, data products, readiness insight, and lower integration drag.

Lead with measurable cycle-time or decision-quality improvement, then explain the AI.

Product companies need a federal integration wrapper.

Vendors bring tools; Sabre can bring mission context, secure adoption, RMF evidence, test, sustainment, and customer trust.

Make Cognition/Devin the first partner proof, then repeat the pattern with data, cloud, cyber, and mission-tech firms.

Clients and customers

Priority account strategy

Start with the accounts where Sabre has access or a credible wedge. Expand only when the pain, money, and contract path are visible.

1

Primary wedge

Air Force / Space Force

Why it matters

EWAAC gives Sabre a concrete Air Force foothold, and the DAF modernization agenda is rich in weapons integration, digital engineering, test, software, and data needs.

First question

Which Eglin or DAF workflows are painful, funded, and measurable enough for a 60-90 day pilot?

Take three offers to Denver: EWAAC digital engineering acceleration, Devin-backed software throughput, and test/evidence automation.
2

Scale field

Fourth Estate / Joint

Why it matters

DISA, DLA, DHA, CDAO, MDA, and other Defense-Wide buyers sit on enterprise pain where software, data, cyber, workflow, and compliance converge.

First question

Where does an enterprise buyer have a slow workflow, a data bottleneck, or an authorization drag that Sabre can shrink quickly?

Pick one Fourth Estate lighthouse account and shape a pilot around secure modernization plus measurable cycle-time reduction.
3

Proof base

Navy / Marine Corps

Why it matters

NAVAIR and Navy work prove Sabre can perform in mission systems, but the growth move is to turn that proof into reusable Navy/Marine digital-thread and sustainment offers.

First question

Which NAVAIR lessons can open NAVSEA, NIWC, PEO Digital, unmanned, shipyard, or Marine Corps conversations?

Harvest NAVAIR/NDD patterns into portable offer language and account-specific proof artifacts.
4

APG expansion

Army

Why it matters

Army C5ISR, tactical network, EW, cyber, lab, and depot modernization are natural extensions if Sabre avoids generic staffing language.

First question

Where do APG/C5ISR relationships expose modernization friction that can be shaped into funded technical work?

Map existing Sabre access at APG, then select two pursuits with buyer, vehicle, partner, and first proof defined.

Sabre alignment

Turn current proof into the next growth motion

Sabre's advantage is not one capability label. It is the ability to combine mission engineering, secure software, data, cyber, contracts, and delivery in environments where pure product companies struggle.

$499M NAWCAD SED award

Sabre has mission-systems credibility in complex Navy aviation environments.

Translate that into digital-thread, test, data, software, and cyber offers for adjacent Navy/Marine buyers.

Prime position on EWAAC

Sabre has a practical entry point into Air Force armament and rapid acquisition work.

Use EWAAC as the first Air Force growth operating lane, not just a contract logo.

APG / C5ISR presence

Sabre can credibly enter Army technical conversations around communications, cyber, integration, and lab modernization.

Package APG access into C5ISR modernization pursuits with clear technical discriminators.

Cognition / Devin relationship

Adam can bring a live commercial wedge into Sabre, but only if it is wrapped for federal adoption.

Make Sabre the SI wrapper: security, RMF evidence, evaluation, human review, deployment, and sustainment.

Execution

First 30 days at Sabre

The outcome is not a deck. The outcome is a qualified growth machine with one Air Force lighthouse, one Fourth Estate lighthouse, and one Devin-backed pilot path.

Days 1-5

Listen and map power

  • Meet Sabre growth, capture, delivery, and contract owners.
  • Map vehicles, incumbents, customer owners, task-order health, and no-go boundaries.
  • Ask where Sabre is credible but not converting credibility into growth.
Days 6-10

Build the market map

  • Create service/Fourth Estate account maps.
  • Tie budget lines to likely buyers, vehicles, and Sabre capabilities.
  • Rank 15 target accounts by access, urgency, funding, partner leverage, and delivery credibility.
Days 11-15

Partner and productize

  • Run Cognition/Devin security and partner diligence.
  • Create partner tiers: platform, mission tech, cyber/data, capture allies, and subcontract channels.
  • Turn generic services into six named offers with proof artifacts.
Days 16-20

Pick lighthouse pursuits

  • Select one Air Force/EWAAC play, one Fourth Estate play, and one Devin pilot.
  • Draft solution sketches, customer questions, vehicle path, differentiators, and delivery handoff.
  • Identify first customer meeting and first internal owner for each.
Days 21-30

Operate the growth machine

  • Stand up a weekly growth architecture board.
  • Publish the pursuit intake and qualification method.
  • Create first brief decks, one-pagers, budget traces, partner asks, and pilot charters.

Program Vehicles and Task Orders
